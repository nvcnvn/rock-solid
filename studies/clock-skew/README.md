# Clock skew: CockroachDB's bounded offset against rock-solid's bounded rate

**Status:** read from source and documentation, no experiment. Recommendations 1, 4 and 5 are adopted in the design; 2 and 3 are open, and each [recommendation](#recommendations) names the sections it touches.

**Question:** CockroachDB runs on commodity clocks, without Spanner's atomic and GPS time, by assuming a bound on the *offset* between any two nodes' wall clocks and then waiting or repeating work around that bound. rock-solid counts every lease as a *duration* on two local clocks and never compares readings across nodes ([Lease time](../../docs/design.md#epoch-changes-and-failure-detection)). Does any of CockroachDB's machinery (hybrid logical clocks, the maximum offset and self-termination, uncertainty restarts, read refresh, commit wait, lease stasis) map onto rock-solid, and would adopting it simplify the design or the code?

## Summary

CockroachDB needs a clock bound because its order *is* time: every transaction reads and writes at a hybrid logical clock (HLC) timestamp picked on its gateway, so two causally ordered transactions on gateways whose clocks disagree could get timestamps in the wrong order. It assumes any two clocks differ by at most `--max-offset` (500 ms by default) and makes up for the rest in two ways. It **repeats**: a read that finds a value timestamped above its own but within `max_offset` cannot tell whether that write came first, so it moves its timestamp up and refreshes or restarts; observed timestamps shrink the window to nearly nothing on nodes it has already visited. It **waits** only where it writes ahead of present time: transactions on global tables commit-wait about 800 ms, an opt-in linearizable mode commit-waits `max_offset` on every write, a restarted node sleeps up to `max_offset`, and each range lease ends `max_offset` early (its stasis period). A node that finds itself more than 80% of `max_offset` away from at least half of its peers kills itself. Past the bound, CockroachDB loses single-key linearizability (stale reads), never serializability, since isolation and Raft's lease checks have no clock dependence.

rock-solid needs none of that, because nothing in it is ordered by time. A chunk's single primary orders its writes as versions (the PG's interval number in the high bits, a counter below), each MetaStore shard is ordered by its Raft log, snapshots are cut by pausing writers, and backups read frozen versions ([ChunkStore](../../docs/design.md#data-layer-chunkstore), [Snapshots](../../docs/design.md#snapshots-and-clones)). Clocks appear in exactly one role: making the end of a lease's holder come before the end of its grantor in real time. That needs only a bound on clock *rate*, which no time service has to provide, which a wall-clock step cannot break, and which nodes check pairwise from their heartbeats. CockroachDB's leasing has been moving the same way: its leader leases ride on Store Liveness, whose correctness its own documentation says does not depend on clock skew or drift, and rock-solid's leader leases already copy that design.

**Verdict: nothing to adopt, and nothing to simplify.** HLCs, uncertainty intervals, read refresh and commit wait each solve a problem rock-solid does not have; taking them on would add a dependency on synchronized clocks, a self-termination path tied to the time service, and per-message clock state, in exchange for features (reads at arbitrary past timestamps, follower reads, global tables) the design does not offer. The current approach is simpler and assumes less. Both designs confine clock dependence to reads: when the clock assumption breaks, each can serve a stale read from an old leaseholder, and neither can apply a conflicting write. Three small things carry over: CockroachDB's way of judging measurements (discard slow round trips, decide by majority), turned toward safety for rock-solid's rate estimate; its handling of VM pauses, which supports the direction of the design's open question; and an HLC-style max rule for the one wall-clock value rock-solid stores, `mtime`.

## CockroachDB

Read at `cockroachdb/cockroach` master `d30c905` (2026-09-23) and `cockroachdb/docs` main `27e64c5` (v26.1 pages). The *Living Without Atomic Clocks* blog post, the 2025 *Clock Management in CockroachDB* post and the SIGMOD 2020 paper could not be fetched from this environment (see [Unverified](#unverified)); the package documentation of `pkg/util/hlc`, which search excerpts show the paper's clock section follows closely, stands in for them.

### Hybrid logical clocks

- Each node keeps an HLC: a physical part from its "coarsely-synchronized system clock" and a logical part, Lamport-style, that breaks ties (`pkg/util/hlc/doc.go:11-14`). Nodes attach readings to Store Liveness messages, to some Raft commands (lease starts, merge freeze times) and to every KV request, response and error, and forward their clock to every reading they receive (`doc.go:25-55`).
- Why: MVCC timestamps are the serialization order, and an HLC keeps that order close to real time while making causality survive a slow clock. A node's timestamps are strictly monotonic, across restarts too, which it enforces "by waiting out the maximum clock offset upon process startup before serving any requests" (`doc.go:169-175`; `pkg/server/clock_monotonicity.go:123`, unless a persisted upper bound, off by default, says less). Forwarding on receipt also makes clocks converge in practice ("self-stabilization"), with no guarantee attached (`doc.go:190-197`).
- Leases lean on it. A lease's start is an HLC reading of the outgoing holder, and the incoming holder forwards its clock to it, so a leaseholder's clock always leads every value a previous leaseholder wrote (`doc.go:81-97`; invariant 2 in `pkg/kv/kvserver/uncertainty/doc.go:203-211`).

### The offset bound and self-termination

- **What it bounds.** `MaxOffset` is "the maximal clock offset to any node in the cluster as specified by the operator", used "to safely hand off leases and enforce single-key linearizability" (`pkg/util/hlc/hlc.go:328-338`). It defaults to 500 ms (`pkg/base/constants.go:15`, whose comment notes Azure offsets of 250 to 500 ms are common); the documentation recommends 250 ms for new multi-region clusters, to cut the write latency of global tables. It must match on every node except during a rolling change, when real skew must stay under the smallest value (`pkg/cli/cliflags/flags.go:1035-1056`).
- **How offsets are measured.** RPC heartbeats every second (`base.PingInterval`) apply Cristian's remote clock reading: offset = remote time + RTT/2 − receive time, with an uncertainty of RTT/2, assuming zero drift; a ping slower than a multiple of the tolerated offset gives no sample (`pkg/rpc/clock_offset.go:535-548`).
- **What happens past it.** The tolerated offset is 80% of `MaxOffset`, "conservatively set ... to avoid exceeding MaxOffset" (`pkg/server/config.go:82-86`). After every sample, `VerifyClockOffset` counts the peers whose offset is within it, counting an ambiguous one (the bound inside the sample's uncertainty) as healthy "on the side of not spuriously killing nodes", and returns an error when no more than half are, upon which the node terminates (`clock_offset.go:410-478`): "this node is more than 400ms away from at least half of the known nodes". The documentation says it "crashes immediately" (`architecture/transaction-layer.md:96`). `--disable-max-offset-check` turns the check off and leaves the bound to the operator.
- **Jumps and VM pauses.** The HLC never runs backwards: it logs a backward jump of the wall clock larger than `max_offset / 10` and keeps its own reading, and, if `server.clock.forward_jump_check_enabled` is set, exits on a forward jump of `max_offset / 2` or more (`hlc.go:254-258`, `hlc.go:385-400`). The documentation admits a stale-clock window after vMotion suspends and resumes a VM, during which a client "could read stale data and write data derived from stale reads"; the forward-jump check only alarms afterwards, and the remedy is `--clock-device`, which reads time from a PTP hardware clock the hypervisor provides (`_includes/v26.1/faq/clock-synchronization-effects.md:5`; `flags.go:1024-1033`).

### Repeating: uncertainty intervals, observed timestamps, read refresh

- **The interval.** A transaction gets a provisional timestamp `ts` from its gateway's HLC and an uncertainty interval `[ts, ts + max_offset]`: since `max_offset` bounds how far ahead the fastest clock can be, any transaction that committed causally before this one has a timestamp at or below the upper limit (`uncertainty/doc.go:80-90`; `hlc/doc.go:214-239`).
- **The restart.** A read that meets a value above `ts` but at or below the limit raises `ReadWithinUncertaintyIntervalError` and moves its timestamp above the value, keeping the upper limit fixed, "through a refresh or a retry" (`uncertainty/doc.go:103-117`). That treats every uncertain value as past, so operations on each key follow real-time order. The documentation calls the error "generally a sign of contention": it needs a write to the same key within `max_offset` of the read (`transaction-retry-error-reference.md:214-219`).
- **Observed timestamps shrink the window.** The first time a transaction visits a node it records a reading of that node's HLC. Because a leaseholder's clock leads every value it and its predecessors wrote, any value on that node with a later *local* timestamp cannot have preceded the transaction, so the local limit there is min(global limit, observation), forwarded to the lease's start (`uncertainty/doc.go:141-258`). In the documentation's words, a retried transaction never meets uncertainty on a node it visited, and never on its gateway (`architecture/transaction-layer.md:308`). Values keep the local timestamp they were written at even when intent resolution moves them to a later commit timestamp, precisely so that this argument holds (`uncertainty/doc.go:189-201`).
- **Non-transactional requests** take both their timestamp and their interval from the leaseholder's clock, so they almost never restart, and when they do the leaseholder retries them itself; they still need an interval, for leases that changed after the timestamp was taken and for intents resolved to a later commit timestamp (`uncertainty/doc.go:260-370`).
- **Read refresh avoids the full restart.** Transactions take pessimistic write locks and optimistic read locks. When a transaction must move its timestamp, the span refresher re-reads every span it has read and checks that no write landed between the old and the new timestamp; if none did, it moves its timestamp without redoing anything, else it restarts. Refresh spans are capped at 4 MiB per transaction (`kvcoord/txn_interceptor_span_refresher.go:28-118`).

### Waiting: commit wait and where CockroachDB pays it

- **Spanner** waits on every read-write transaction: the coordinator leader waits until TrueTime says the commit timestamp is past, "at least 2ε", overlapped with Paxos (Spanner §4.2.1 as quoted in `docs/RFCS/20200811_non_blocking_txns.md:626-640`). The blog post puts Spanner's bound at 7 ms (search excerpt only).
- **CockroachDB by default** never waits after a commit. It guarantees only that every clock is past *commit timestamp − `max_offset`* when a commit is acknowledged, which holds trivially for timestamps at or below present time, and makes up the difference with uncertainty intervals on later readers (`non_blocking_txns.md:641-651`). Spanner needs no intervals because its writers hold locks through commit wait; CockroachDB releases them first (`kvcoord/txn_coord_sender.go:656-670`).
- **Where it does wait:**
  - *Future-time writes.* Non-blocking transactions on global tables write ahead of present time so that followers can serve consistent reads; the closed-timestamp lead is propagation time + `max_offset` + 25 ms, "800ms" by default (`kvserver/closedts/policy.go:20-104`), and writers commit-wait until their HLC passes the commit timestamp (`txn_coord_sender.go:685-698`; `global-tables.md:79`). A reader that bumps its timestamp to such a write waits out the uncertainty before it reads at the new timestamp (`non_blocking_txns.md:676-760`).
  - *Linearizable mode.* `COCKROACH_EXPERIMENTAL_LINEARIZABLE` makes every writing transaction wait a further `max_offset`, 500 ms by default, so that causally later transactions get later timestamps whatever their gateway; the code calls this mode "completely untested" (`hlc/doc.go:291-317`; `txn_coord_sender.go:690-692`). The design document's alternative is causality tokens passed by the client (`docs/design.md:524-553`).
  - *Restarts.* Up to `max_offset` before a restarted node serves (above).
  - *Leases.* The stasis period, below.

### Leases

- **Three kinds.** Expiration leases (6 s, renewed by a Raft command halfway through), epoch leases tied to a node-liveness record (6 s, renewed by a conditional put per node) and, newest, leader leases on Store Liveness (3 s of support, renewed by pairwise heartbeats). The default-config comment tabulates failover: 3.01 to 6.40 s for expiration leases, 3.03 to 7.20 s for epoch leases, 2.04 to 6.70 s for leader leases including the election (`pkg/base/config.go:148-199`).
- **Stasis.** A lease is UNUSABLE for the last `max_offset` before its expiration: a read at `T` must see every write in `[T, T + max_offset]`, and without the stasis "a new leaseholder with a fast clock could start performing writes ordered in real time before the old leaseholder considers its lease to have expired" (`kvserverpb/lease_status.proto:42-75`; `leases/status.go:195-212`). With 500 ms on a 6 s lease, that is 8.3% of each lease. It doubles as a catch-all uncertainty interval for requests that carry none (`leases/status.go:76-98`). The epoch-lease RFC states the same rule: the holder's liveness expiration must be at least `max_offset` beyond the command's timestamp (`docs/RFCS/20160210_range_leases.md:72`).
- **Leader leases on Store Liveness.** Stores heartbeat each other pairwise; a supporter grants support for an epoch until an expiration taken from the *requester's* clock, withdraws it once its own clock passes that expiration, and bumps the epoch so support never returns (`storeliveness/doc.go:38-66`). A Raft leader is *fortified* once a quorum supports it, and its lease lasts to the quorum's earliest expiration (`doc.go:146-160`). Expirations are timestamps "from the MVCC domain, and not from the real time domain", so the holder and supporter agree that the promise covers proposals below `t` whatever their real clocks say (`doc.go:167-175`), and "clock skew and drift do not affect the correctness of store liveness", only its liveness, provided each message forwards the receiver's HLC; without that forwarding, two epochs' support could overlap (`doc.go:127-133`; `storelivenesspb/service.proto:48-68`). A restarted store waits out the support it requested before its restart (`hlc/doc.go:146-167`).

### Guarantees, and what breaks past the bound

| Guarantee | Clock dependence | Past `max_offset` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Serializability | None: "transaction isolation has no clock dependence" | Holds (stated as conjecture in `hlc.go:333-335`, "not ... conclusively verified") |
| Single-key linearizability | Uncertainty intervals and lease stasis | Stale reads: a transaction on a slow gateway misses a write its client already saw committed (`hlc/doc.go:241-251`) |
| Lease exclusivity | In MVCC time, none; in real time, the stasis | Two replicas may both believe they hold the lease, but the lease sequence is checked at Raft application, so their writes cannot conflict; non-transactional reads can be stale (`hlc/doc.go:253-289`) |
| Strict serializability (no causal reverse) | Not offered by default; linearizable mode or causality tokens | Even with commit wait, causal reverse returns (`hlc/doc.go:291-317`) |

## rock-solid

### What its clocks are for

| Use | Clock | Compared across nodes? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Node-pair leases behind primary read leases and Raft leader fortification | Holder: `CLOCK_BOOTTIME` and `CLOCK_MONOTONIC_RAW` from its request, ends `lease_drift` early on the faster; grantor: both from its grant, keeps it until the slower shows the whole lease | No: durations only |
| Session leases | Same rule, client as holder, control-plane leader as grantor | No |
| Rate check between nodes | Heartbeats carry the sender's `CLOCK_MONOTONIC_RAW`; each node estimates peers' rates | Differences of readings only, never offsets |
| Restart wait | One `primary_lease` before a restarted node votes or peers | No |
| Failure detection, member timeouts, recall timeouts | Timers on `CLOCK_BOOTTIME` | No |
| Incarnation | The daemon's start time on `CLOCK_BOOTTIME` | Only against another incarnation of the same session, which a successor on the same node in the same boot inherits; a reboot loses the memfd that carries the session, so a new boot always starts a new session |
| `mtime` | Not stated in the design | Yes, if each client node stamps it (see [recommendation 4](#recommendations)) |

Ordering never comes from a clock. Chunk versions are a logical counter scoped to the PG's interval, so they "grow across primaries" without any physical time; the MetaStore orders by Raft index; cross-shard transactions commit at one record key, with no timestamp service; the snapshot cut pauses writers and resumes them under a new snapshot epoch, and the [Alternatives considered](../../docs/design.md#alternatives-considered) table already rejects HLC-timestamped multi-version chunks for it.

### Side by side

| | CockroachDB | rock-solid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What is assumed | Any two wall clocks within `max_offset` (500 ms) | Each lease's two counting clocks run within `lease_drift` (1%) of each other's rate, after each side takes its safe clock |
| Who provides it | The operator's time service (NTP, chrony, a cloud time service, or PTP) | The oscillators (±100 ppm each); a time daemon only slows or speeds a clock, which the two-clock rule absorbs |
| Breaks it | A time-service outage long enough for drift to add up, a misconfigured source, a step, a VM pause | A VM pause; two clocks of one node miscalibrated the same way and missed by the heartbeat check |
| Checked by | Offset samples per heartbeat; minority node self-terminates at 80% | Rate estimates per node pair; holder widens its margin for a fast grantor and reports the pair |
| Steady cost | Stasis: last 500 ms of every lease unusable; uncertainty restarts under contention; 500 ms wait at startup | 1% of each lease (40 ms of 4 s); one `primary_lease` (4 s) wait after restart |
| Cost under a time-daemon slew | None (a slew keeps offsets small) | A grantor waits longer, at most 4 s / (1 − 0.226) = 5.2 s for a 4 s lease during the most extreme slew the kernel accepts |
| Crash failover | 2.0 to 6.7 s for leader leases | `primary_lease` (4 s) plus a 1 to 2 s election for Raft groups; `node_down_after` (5 s) for data-path writes |
| Past the assumption | Stale reads; no write conflicts | Stale reads from an old primary or leader; peering and Raft terms still stop any write from an old interval or term, and the session fence still rejects a fenced client's writes |

Two numbers show the gap between the assumptions. Two free-running oscillators at the design's ±100 ppm drift apart by 200 ppm, so with the time service gone they cross 500 ms after 2,500 s (42 minutes), and the recommended 250 ms after 21 minutes, at which point CockroachDB starts losing nodes; rock-solid's margin covers that rate fifty times over and never needs the time service at all. And a wall-clock step of any size, which shifts CockroachDB's offsets at once, moves neither of rock-solid's clocks.

## What carries over

| CockroachDB technique | Problem it solves there | In rock-solid | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HLC on every message | Causal order of MVCC timestamps picked on different nodes | No timestamps to order; chunk versions and Raft indexes already give one order per chunk and per shard | Does not apply. It would add 12 bytes and a clock update to every message and a monotonicity problem across restarts, for no consumer |
| `max_offset` with self-termination | Keeps the HLC's premise true | No offset premise exists | Does not apply. Adopting it would tie availability to the time service |
| Uncertainty intervals and read restarts ("repeat") | A gateway-chosen read timestamp may be below a causally earlier write | Reads go to the one primary or leader holding a lease in real time and return its newest committed version, so there is no window of doubt | Does not apply. Restarts would also break "readers never wait for writers" |
| Observed timestamps | Shrink the uncertainty window | No window | Does not apply |
| Read refresh | Move a transaction's timestamp without redoing its reads | Transactions have no timestamp to move; a cross-shard transaction's intents are its locks | Does not apply |
| Commit wait ("wait") | Real-time order for writes in the future (global tables) or across disjoint keys (linearizable mode) | No reads at a timestamp, so nothing to wait out; the design's waits are the grantor's lease wait, the restart wait and the session fence | Does not apply |
| Lease stasis of `max_offset` | Old and new leaseholders' real-time overlap under offset | The holder ends its count first by construction; the margin is `lease_drift` | Already covered, and cheaper (1% against 8.3%) |
| Leader leases on Store Liveness: pairwise support, epochs bumped on withdrawal, restarted store waits out its requests | Lease cost independent of range count; no central liveness record | Already the design: node-pair leases, support epochs, fortification, restart wait | Already adopted, with durations in place of HLC expirations, since rock-solid has no MVCC time domain in which to argue disjointness |
| Wait `max_offset` on restart, or persist an HLC upper bound | Monotonic timestamps across restarts | One `primary_lease` wait, because a restarted node forgot its grants | Keep as is. Persisting grants would save 4 s only on a daemon restart within one boot (`CLOCK_BOOTTIME` resets at reboot), and CockroachDB ships its own persisted bound off by default |
| Discard slow samples; judge by majority | Turn noisy offset samples into a kill decision without killing healthy nodes | Rate estimate per pair, "reports the pair" | Carries over, turned toward safety (recommendations 1 and 2) |
| `--clock-device` on a PTP hardware clock for vMotion | Stale clock after a VM pause | Open question: a holder that resumes from a VM pause still trusts its leases | Carries over as a direction for the open question (recommendation 3) |
| HLC's max rule (forward to the largest time seen) | Monotonic timestamps despite slow clocks | `mtime` written from client nodes | Carries over in miniature (recommendation 4) |

## Recommendations

None of these changes the lease design. Each names what it would touch.

1. **Make the rate estimate err toward safety, and say how it is computed.** CockroachDB treats an ambiguous offset sample as healthy, because its action, self-termination, is costly. rock-solid's action, widening a holder's margin for one grantor, costs a few milliseconds of lease, so it should take the conservative end: widen when the grantor's rate *may* exceed `lease_drift / 2` faster, using the upper bound of the estimate, not its mean. The estimate also needs a stated window and a rule for noisy samples. Over a window *W*, round-trip jitter *J* bounds the relative-rate error by about 2*J*/*W*: 1 ms of jitter over 10 s is 200 ppm, against a 5,000 ppm threshold, so a 10 s window of heartbeats, dropping samples whose round trip exceeds a few times the median (as CockroachDB drops slow pings), is ample. Touches: [Lease time](../../docs/design.md#epoch-changes-and-failure-detection) (one sentence), the *Peering and read leases* item of `docs/build-checklist.md`, and the future `internal/lease`; a simulator scenario with heartbeat jitter next to the existing oscillator drift in *Simulation faults for placement* ([Testing strategy](../../docs/design.md#testing-strategy)).
2. **Name the miscalibrated node by majority.** A holder that widens its margin "reports the pair", but a pair cannot say which of its two oscillators is off. CockroachDB's rule fits: the control plane, which already receives every node's heartbeat, flags a node whose rate disagrees with more than half of its peers by more than `lease_drift / 2` and raises an alert naming it. It should not mark the node down: unlike CockroachDB, rock-solid stays safe while margins widen, so the alert is for the operator, not for correctness. Touches: [Lease time](../../docs/design.md#epoch-changes-and-failure-detection), and the metrics the control plane exports.
3. **Point the VM-pause open question at a host clock.** CockroachDB faces the same hazard ([open question](../../docs/design.md#roadmap-and-open-questions) on leases across a VM pause) and documents no software fix: its forward-jump check alarms after the fact, and its remedy is reading time from a PTP clock the hypervisor exposes. For rock-solid the need is different, detecting that a pause happened rather than reading the right time, but the same device could serve: a host-backed PTP clock (`ptp_kvm`, or the VMClock device the question already names) read beside `CLOCK_BOOTTIME` each pass would show a pause as a gap, the way `Resumed` shows a suspend. Whether such a clock keeps host time across a pause, a snapshot restore and a live migration, and what it costs per read, needs a spike. Touches: the open question's text in [Roadmap and open questions](../../docs/design.md#roadmap-and-open-questions).
4. **Say whose clock stamps `mtime`, and keep it from going backwards.** The write path commits "size, mtime" at `close` and `fsync` ([Client design](../../docs/design.md#client-design)) without naming the clock. If each client node stamps it from its own `CLOCK_REALTIME`, two nodes writing one file in turn can leave an `mtime` older than the previous one, which confuses `make` and `rsync`. An HLC-style rule in the inode's transaction, new `mtime` = max(client's time, stored `mtime` + 1 ns) for implicit updates, with an explicit `utimensat` kept as given, keeps it monotonic per inode at no cost. How other shared filesystems source `mtime`, and how FUSE's writeback-cache mode interacts, was not read for this study. Touches: [Client design](../../docs/design.md#client-design) write path.
5. **Record the comparison where the design rejects synchronized clocks.** The *Lease clock* row of [Alternatives considered](../../docs/design.md#alternatives-considered) already lists "synchronized clocks with a maximum offset (CockroachDB)" without a reason; its reason column could carry this study's: a 500 ms stasis per lease against 1%, nodes that self-terminate when the time service fails for about 40 minutes, and the same stale-read exposure past the bound.

- **When `--max-offset` changed.** The source at master has a 500 ms default, a separate tolerated offset at 80% and `--disable-max-offset-check`; the documentation recommends 250 ms for new multi-region clusters. Which release split the tolerated offset from `max_offset` was not checked.
- **CockroachDB's leader-lease failover figures** are the generated comment in `pkg/base/config.go`, not a measurement.
- **The rate-estimate arithmetic** (2*J*/*W*) assumes jitter that is independent across samples and no asymmetric path change inside a window; a simulator scenario should check it.
- **Host PTP clocks across a VM pause** (recommendation 3) are untested: whether `ptp_kvm` or VMClock keep counting across QEMU and Firecracker pauses, snapshot restores and migrations, and their read cost.
- **`mtime` in other systems** (recommendation 4) was not read.
- **Branch heads, not releases.** CockroachDB and its documentation were read at their default branches on 2026-10-19; the commits are pinned in `fetch.sh`.
