# LSM study: durability spike

**Question.** Do the disk log's group commit, record header, tail rule, checkpoints and manifest follow what Pebble and RocksDB do for their write-ahead log (WAL), commit pipeline, MANIFEST, recovery and memtable flushes, and where they differ, which of their practices should replace or extend ours? In particular: do they keep more than one sync in flight (our `commit_depth`), how do they tell a torn tail from corruption, and should the manifest become incremental edits plus periodic snapshots instead of carrying the whole segment usage table every time?

**Setup.** A source study plus one measurement.

- Sources (read, not built): Pebble master at [a7b8321871e3](https://github.com/cockroachdb/pebble/tree/a7b8321871e3) (2026-10-14), checked against v2.1.7 where it matters; RocksDB [v11.8.1](https://github.com/facebook/rocksdb/tree/v11.8.1). `./fetch.sh` makes the same shallow clones under `work/src`. Citations are `file:line` at those refs.
- Ours: [design.md "On-disk format"](../../docs/design.md#on-disk-format) and `internal/disklog` (`groupcommit.go`, `manifest.go`, `recovery.go`, `record.go`).
- Measurement: [manifestsim](manifestsim/main.go) models a disk at segment granularity (live blocks per segment and temperature class; an overwrite kills a block chosen uniformly among live blocks with a Fenwick tree; a greedy cleaner interleaved with user writes keeps 8 segments free) and checkpoints every 64 MiB of log writes, user and cleaner. Per checkpoint it counts the usage rows that exist (the full table every manifest carries today) and the rows that changed since the last checkpoint (what an edit would carry), and replays two snapshot rules over that sequence: Pebble's `RotationHelper` and RocksDB's `max_manifest_space_amp_pct` at its default 500. It ignores record headers and the runs part of the manifest (tens of KiB). Counts, not wall time, so the shared VM does not matter.

```
./fetch.sh     # sources under work/src (only to check citations)
./run.sh       # about 25 minutes on one shared core -> results-manifest.txt
```

Raw output: [results-manifest.txt](results-manifest.txt).

### Record format

| Durable watermark in each header | **WAL sync chunks** (format `FormatWALSyncChunks`): each chunk header carries `syncedOffset`, the offset the log had synced when the chunk was written ([record.go:87-100](https://github.com/cockroachdb/pebble/blob/a7b8321871e3/record/record.go#L87-L100); written at log_writer.go:1071-1092). | Checksum | CRC32C (Castagnoli) per chunk; sstable blocks CRC32C or xxHash64 | CRC32C per WAL record; SST blocks default `kXXH3` (table.h:374) | CRC32C per 4 KiB block and per header, hardware-accelerated (167 ns per 4 KiB) | **CONFIRM** |

An edit's rows track the user blocks per checkpoint: 3,071 rows at the median in M2 against 16,384 ÷ 5.20 = 3,151 user blocks, 3,164 in M6 on 16× the segments, and about a sixteenth of that with 64 KiB writes (M4, M7). The full table follows the segment count: by the same arithmetic a 16 TiB disk (262,144 × 64 MiB) carries 4.35 MiB, 6.6%, in every manifest, as M6 does. With Pebble's rule a checkpoint costs about twice an edit, and recovery reads at most two snapshots.

## Conclusions

1. **Keep `commit_depth` 1 and the prefix rule.** Pebble and RocksDB both keep one sync in flight per log and get their throughput from the group collected while it runs ([log_writer.go:601-823](https://github.com/cockroachdb/pebble/blob/a7b8321871e3/record/log_writer.go#L601-L823), [db_impl_write.cc:2350-2385](https://github.com/facebook/rocksdb/blob/v11.8.1/db/db_impl/db_impl_write.cc#L2350-L2385)). The only place Pebble writes two devices at once, WAL failover, rewrites every unsynced record and completes waiters in order, so durability stays a prefix, which is our rule. A second round in flight remains a measurement on the NVMe drive, not a practice to copy.
2. **Write the durable LSN into every record header**, as Pebble writes `syncedOffset` into every WAL chunk ([record.go:87-100](https://github.com/cockroachdb/pebble/blob/a7b8321871e3/record/record.go#L87-L100), [466-588](https://github.com/cockroachdb/pebble/blob/a7b8321871e3/record/record.go#L466-L588)), and add to the tail rule: a gap is corruption if any record header found past it names a durable LSN at or past the gap. It catches damage the `commit_depth` window lets through whenever a round began after the damaged one was durable, which at queue depth 1 is every round, and it does not depend on the configured depth. 8 bytes in a 48-byte prelude.
3. **Keep the tail rule's philosophy and its manifest condition.** It matches Pebble's (tolerate an unclean end only without proof of sync) and RocksDB's `kTolerateCorruptedTailRecords`, and its "no segment header names a later manifest" is RocksDB's check that no SST holds data past the corruption ([db_impl_open.cc:1808-1850](https://github.com/facebook/rocksdb/blob/v11.8.1/db/db_impl/db_impl_open.cc#L1808-L1850)). RocksDB's default `kPointInTimeRecovery` and `kSkipAnyCorruptedRecords` drop acknowledged writes; a replica must not rejoin that way.
4. **Make manifests incremental: an edit per checkpoint, a snapshot by Pebble's rule** ([rotation.go:40-68](https://github.com/cockroachdb/pebble/blob/a7b8321871e3/record/rotation.go#L40-L68)). The full usage table costs 0.415% of the log per TiB at 64 MiB segments (M2), 1.66% at 4 TiB (M7) and 6.6% at 16 TiB or at 4 MiB segments (M6); edits plus snapshots cost 0.01 to 0.2% on every disk measured, and recovery reads at most two snapshots. RocksDB's 500% rule writes a third less but lets recovery read six snapshots.
5. **Stop producers while a checkpoint lags**, as Pebble does at `MemTableStopWritesThreshold` 2 and RocksDB at `max_write_buffer_number` 2: otherwise nothing bounds the memtables and retained Raft entries that `max_replay_bytes` is meant to bound.
6. **Verify run contents in release builds where it is cheap**: entry counts on every flush and merge (RocksDB's `flush_verify_memtable_count` and `compaction_verify_record_count`, on by default) and a CRC32C per in-memory run piece, checked when a merge reads it, since runs never come back from disk to be verified as Pebble's and RocksDB's compaction inputs do. Keep the decode check on write as a test and simulator setting, like Pebble's `invariants` builds and RocksDB's `paranoid_file_checks`.
7. **Confirmed as they are:** one owner loop per disk (every committer joins the next group in both engines; CockroachDB runs every range of a store through one Pebble WAL), the 1 MiB round cap (RocksDB's `max_write_batch_group_size_bytes`), write then flush, headers that name their place (recyclable log numbers, RocksDB's context checksums), zeroing the headers past the end, the segment-header pointer as `CURRENT`, the root piece as an atomic group, one checkpoint for every index (`atomic_flush`), and CRC32C.
