# LSM study: deletions

**Question.** Do the range removals the [LSM runs spike](../lsm-runs/) adopted (non-overlapping fragments in the memtable, a write cutting its key out of a fragment, a run's fragments in blocks of their own that its own entries win over, the get/floor/scan rules, a flush or merge keeping a fragment only while an older run holds a key in it) match how Pebble and RocksDB implement range deletions, and what do those engines do about deletions that our tree lacks: dropping whole files a range deletion covers (delete-only compactions, excise, `DeleteFilesInRange`), compactions triggered by tombstone density, `SingleDelete`, and dropping point deletions that reach the oldest data? How should the deletion of a whole chunk, a whole volume or a snapshot be expressed, and do those engines answer the "Cleaner copies in a rebuild" [open question](../../docs/design.md#roadmap-and-open-questions)?

**Setup.** Linux 6.18.44 in a Firecracker VM, 4 vCPUs of an Intel Xeon shared with six other agents' benchmarks, Go 1.27.0. Sources read: Pebble master at [`a7b8321871e3`](https://github.com/cockroachdb/pebble/tree/a7b8321871e3) (2026-10-14) and v2.1.7, RocksDB [v11.8.1](https://github.com/facebook/rocksdb/tree/v11.8.1), the RocksDB wiki pages [DeleteRange Implementation](https://github.com/facebook/rocksdb/wiki/DeleteRange-Implementation) and [Implement Queue Service Using RocksDB](https://github.com/facebook/rocksdb/wiki/Implement-Queue-Service-Using-RocksDB), and Titan's [blob_gc_job.cc](https://github.com/tikv/titan/blob/master/src/blob_gc_job.cc). Written against rock-solid `86c47be`; the docs moved to universal compaction and per-run Bloom filters (`5a5f5e2`) while it ran, which `lsmd` (a copy of the LSM runs spike's tree, with Cassandra's buckets and no filters) does not model.

- [pebblex/](pebblex/main.go) drives Pebble v2.1.7 on an in-memory filesystem and counts with its own iterator and compaction metrics. **P1** is the LSM runs spike's case B on Pebble: 20,000 chunks of four extents and one chunk of 1,024 one-block extents, under the chunk index's 38-byte keys, compacted to L6, then the chunk rewritten whole with 1,023 point deletions or one range deletion; each floor (`SeekLT` of the key's successor) of the chunk's 1,024 blocks counts the points it steps over, in the memtable, after a flush and after a full compaction. **P2** drops one of four MetaStore-like volumes (`fs/{volume}/{inode}/attr`, 250,000 files each, 100-byte values, 256 KiB tables) with point deletions, a range deletion, and a range deletion with delete-only excises, and counts compactions by kind and the bytes they write. **P3** is a queue (append at the tail, delete the head, 64 KiB memtable) beside 200,000 cold keys, with and without tombstone-density compactions, counting the points a seek to the head steps over.
- [lsmd/](lsmd/) is a copy of the LSM runs spike's `lsmx` with one addition, [`DropCovered`](lsmd/drop.go): every run drops the blocks that the fragments of newer layers cover whole and keeps its place in age order, the analogue of Pebble's delete-only compaction. It still writes the run again with the blocks it keeps, since our runs do not yet share pieces. [ours/](ours/main.go) runs P2 and P3 on it: **O1** drops one of four 250,000-file volumes with one range removal and then flushes 400 batches of 2,000 creates in another volume; **O2** is P3's queue, with point removals or with one range removal of the consumed prefix.
- Correctness: the LSM runs spike's map-model test, with `DropCovered` called at random steps (60 seeds, five configurations, 3,000 steps each); a planted bug (dropping a block whose first key alone is covered) must fail.

```
./fetch.sh   # optional: the sources cited, shallow at the versions read
./run.sh     # about 10 minutes on the shared VM; results-tests.txt, results-pebble.txt, results-ours.txt
```

Expected before the run: Pebble shows the same pathology as our tree (point deletions over an old run cost a step per tombstone until a compaction reaches the old data; a range deletion costs two points per floor); a delete-only compaction frees the covered tables without writing anything; dropping covered blocks frees a dropped volume's runs at the first flush, but without piece sharing it rewrites the rest of each run; range removal of a queue's consumed prefix keeps a seek to its head at one step.

## Results

Raw output: [results-pebble.txt](results-pebble.txt), [results-ours.txt](results-ours.txt), [results-tests.txt](results-tests.txt).

### Measurements

| Test | Result |
| --- | --- |
| P1 Pebble, 1,024-block rewrite, point deletions | **1,025 points per floor in the memtable and 1,025 after the flush (119 and 162 µs per floor)**; 1 point (10 µs) only once a full compaction reaches L6 and drops them |
| P1 Pebble, one range deletion | **2 points per floor (6.7 µs in the memtable, 18.6 µs flushed)**, 0 points iterated as covered: the merging iterator seeks lower levels past the tombstone. The same shape as our tree (255/201 µs against 1.8/1.7 µs in the LSM runs spike) |
| P2 Pebble, one volume of four dropped | Point deletions: 11 tombstone-density compactions rewrote 1.02 MB, live bytes 11.8 to 9.4 MB; range deletion: **1 delete-only compaction, 0 bytes written**, live bytes 11.8 to 9.65 MB (the tables straddling the volume's boundaries stay); excises changed nothing here |
| P3 Pebble queue | **About 6,350 points per seek to the head, 9,700 at worst, with or without tombstone-density compactions**; none ran (the bottom level is excluded and the tombstones never filled 10% of a table's blocks) |
| O1 our tree, one volume of four dropped (112 MB of runs, 5 runs) | Without dropping: 112 MB of runs until a merge reached the old runs, between 30 and 100 flushes later under `lsmd`'s buckets (under universal compaction, when the size-amplification or size-ratio rule reaches the run holding the volume). **With `DropCovered`: 90.1 MB right after the flush that wrote the fragment** (22 of 28 MB freed; partly covered blocks stay); but it rewrote **85.6 MB** of kept blocks, as much as the merge it saves, because a run cannot name another run's pieces |
| O2 our tree queue, point removals | **10,820 removals stepped over per seek to the head on average, 12,000 at worst**: the removals of keys flushed before their removal live until a merge reaches them |
| O2 our tree queue, the consumed prefix removed as one range | **1 step per seek** |
| Correctness | `DropCovered` model test passes 300 of 300 runs (4,510 blocks dropped, 5,249 merges); the planted bug fails 300 of 300 |

## Conclusions

1. **Our range removal semantics are the engines' with the sequence numbers taken out.** Fragments in their own blocks, a get that stops at a covering fragment, a scan that moves older layers past the fragment's end and a floor that moves them below its start are what Pebble and RocksDB do. A run's own entries winning over its fragments is Pebble's rule for an ingested table, whose points and range deletions share one sequence number: a fragment sits just below its own run's entries and above every older run. Merging a layer's overlapping fragments into their union replaces the seqnum stacks, which exist for snapshots our views do not need.
2. **Pebble pays the same price for point deletions over old data** (P1: 1,025 points and 119 to 162 µs per floor until a full compaction, against 2 points and 7 to 19 µs with a range deletion). The LSM runs spike's fix is the one Pebble's users apply.
3. **The prototype's data structures do not carry over.** A memtable's fragments belong in a second copy-on-write page tree in the memtable's page store (Pebble and RocksDB keep a second skiplist), since a block volume's deletion is one fragment per chunk and the prototype's slice costs O(fragments) and an allocation per operation; a scan keeps each layer's current fragment instead of searching every layer's fragments at every step; and a flush or merge decides whether to keep a removal with a forward cursor per older run rather than a search per run per removal.
4. **Whole blocks under a fragment can be dropped without a merge** (O1: 22 of 28 MB freed at the first flush, model test clean, planted bug caught), as Pebble's delete-only compactions drop whole tables. It pays only when the trimmed run names the pieces it keeps: without that it rewrote 85.6 MB, as much as the merge it replaced.
5. **Density triggers are not needed now; head-of-range removal is.** Neither Pebble's density heuristic (P3) nor ours (the LSM runs spike) helped the cases measured; consuming a key range from its head with one range removal of the consumed prefix keeps a seek at one step (O2).
6. **`SingleDelete`, `DeleteFilesInRange` and excise do not transfer**: exact elision already gives `SingleDelete`'s gain, and replay duplicates writes, which `SingleDelete` forbids; whole-file drops that ignore snapshots have no place beside point-in-time views.
7. **A relocation applies only where the index still points at what was copied** (Titan's GC write callback). A cleaner copy entry that names its source location applies the same in live application, replay and rebuild.
