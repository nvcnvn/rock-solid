# LSM space, flow control and background work

**Question.** How do Pebble and RocksDB make sure a compaction has room, react when the disk fills, slow writers when flushes or compactions fall behind, and pace background writes and file deletion, and which of those practices should the per-disk log adopt? In particular, the open question from the [cleaner streams spike](../cleaner-streams/): a compaction holds its inputs until a manifest names its output, and a MetaStore of 2% of the disk ran a 90% full disk out of free segments with an 8-segment reserve. Does compaction write its output in pieces that free input pieces as they go, or does the reserve grow with the largest run? And how are writers slowed when compaction or cleaning falls behind, and how is the TRIM of freed segments paced?

## Setup

Source study, plus a deterministic model of the metadata stream.

- **Sources** (fetched by [`fetch.sh`](fetch.sh) into `work/src`): Pebble master at [`a7b8321871e3`](https://github.com/cockroachdb/pebble/tree/a7b8321871e3) (2026-10-14) and v2.1.7; RocksDB v11.8.1; CockroachDB at [`ea997fe3b5fb`](https://github.com/cockroachdb/cockroach/tree/ea997fe3b5fb) for the Pebble options it sets, its admission control and ballast; ScyllaDB at [`780d9fef0ba7`](https://github.com/scylladb/scylladb/tree/780d9fef0ba7) for incremental compaction, the size-tiered strategy that frees inputs during a compaction; Linux v6.12 for asynchronous discard.
- **Model** ([`sim/main.go`](sim/main.go), Go 1.27, no dependencies, deterministic). One disk's metadata stream: every checkpoint flushes each index's memtable as a run of uniformly spread keys (distinct keys `K(1 − e^(−u/K))` for `u` updates), compaction picks buckets with [internal/lsm/compact.go](../../internal/lsm/compact.go)'s rule over runs adjacent in age as of `c14f6e1`, optionally (**+amp**) with RocksDB universal's size amplification rule first: merge every run once the newer ones hold twice the oldest. While this study ran, the design adopted universal compaction ([LSM compaction study](../lsm-study-compaction/)), whose first rule is that one, so the +amp rows are the design's case. Pieces of 1 MiB fill segments, a manifest of 17 B per used segment of a 90% full disk follows, and a greedy cleaner copies metadata segments at most half live, skipping segments that hold inputs of a running compaction, while more than 4 segments are stranded. Compaction output is installed **whole** (today's design: written by the checkpoint after the merge ends, inputs dead at that manifest) or in **steps** of N MiB, each step's pieces, the output's new root and a manifest written together, the inputs bounded below by the step's last key so their pieces wholly below it die at that manifest. **+own**: compaction output fills a metadata segment of its own instead of sharing the open one with flushes and manifests. A worker merges 512 MiB of output per checkpoint interval (32 MiB on the stand-in).
- **Scenarios.** S1: the cleaner streams spike's stand-in, a 16 GiB disk of 4,096 segments of 4 MiB, MetaStore index of 2% of the disk (328 MiB of 100 B entries, 2 MiB flushes), 4,000 checkpoints. R1/R2: a 256 GiB disk of 4,096 segments of 64 MiB, MetaStore index of 2% (5.1 GiB) or 6.25% (16 GiB, the largest shard the design plans for), 32 MiB flushes, a 1 GB chunk index with 2 MiB flushes, one worker. R3: eight 1% shards with 4 MiB flushes and two workers. The first fifth of each run is warm-up.
- **Measured:** bytes held beyond `max(bytes the durable manifest names, bytes the next one names)` at the moment each manifest is written, which is what must fit in free segments while ordinary writes are refused; metadata fragmentation; bytes written per flushed byte.

## Conclusions

1. **Compaction installs its output in steps, as ScyllaDB's incremental compaction does.** Whole, the peak a compaction holds beyond what the manifests name is its whole output: 69 segments on the stand-in, where the cleaner streams spike ran out, and 65 to 258 segments of 64 MiB on a 256 GiB disk, up to the size of the largest index whenever universal compaction's size amplification rule merges every run. No fixed reserve covers that, and a reserve that grows with the largest run costs up to 6% of the disk. In steps of 64 MiB, each ending with a manifest that bounds the inputs below the step's last key, the peak is one step plus a checkpoint's flush, 2 segments, for 0.3 to 0.6% more manifest bytes per flushed byte. Runs need no sequence numbers for this: the output covers the keys below the bound and the bounded inputs the keys at or above it.
2. **Compaction output fills a metadata segment of its own.** Steps free input pieces in key order; in shared segments they die among live flush pieces, and fragmentation averaged 30 to 57 segments instead of 8 to 10.
3. **Only work that frees space uses the reserve:** checkpoints, the cleaner and compaction steps, each of which frees at least what it writes once its manifest is durable. With steps, a reserve of `⌈(2 × max_replay_bytes + workers × step) ÷ segment⌉ + victims per pass` segments is 7 on a 256 GiB disk with one worker, inside today's 8. RocksDB reserves the same three things: the write buffer, each compaction's inputs, and a buffer.
4. **Writers slow, then wait, rather than fail while the cleaner can still free space.** Cleaning debt is free segments below the cleaner's target; at RocksDB's L0 proportions (trigger 4, speedup 8, slowdown 20, stop 36) a second cleaner worker joins an eighth of the way down, chunk writes slow half way, and ordinary writes wait at none. The slowed rate comes from what the cleaner measurably frees, as CockroachDB's admission control sets write tokens from bytes compacted out of L0 (and sets Pebble's own L0 stop to 1,000), not from a fixed rate (RocksDB's `delayed_write_rate`) or a stop alone (Pebble's). `NoSpace` remains for a disk the cleaner cannot free.
5. **Replay debt stops writes at twice `max_replay_bytes`**, Pebble's and RocksDB's memtable stop, which the design adopted from the durability study while this one ran; it also bounds a checkpoint's size for the reserve. A run-count stop (Pebble's L0, RocksDB's L0 slowdown) is not needed: runs live in memory, a run-limit merge runs beside a large one, and an extra run costs a lookup well under a microsecond, not space.
6. **TRIM is paced, never accelerated for space.** A freed segment is free in the usage table before its TRIM runs, so trimming frees no log space, unlike deleting a file under Pebble; only the drive's latency is at stake. One TRIM per segment at a baseline of 128 MiB/s, with a backlog older than 5 minutes cleared within 5 minutes; reusing a segment drops its pending TRIM; a file-backed disk never trims, since punching holes would undo writing the file in full.
7. **Not adopted:** a background I/O rate limiter (rounds already alternate streams, and Pebble dropped compaction pacing), polling free space (the owner sees every freed segment), and a size cap on a compaction instead of steps (it leaves size-tiered runs unmerged above the cap).
8. **Caveats.** The model writes no data stream and its metadata cleaner is greedy and runs once per checkpoint: run after every step manifest, it copied 60% more in R3, cleaning segments whose input pieces were about to die. Keys are uniform; the picker has universal compaction's size amplification rule but not its size ratio rule or run limit, which change how often merges happen, not how much one holds.
